Back to the Drawing Board:
A Critical Evaluation of Few-Step Solvers for Production Diffusion Models

1City University of Hong Kong 2City University of Hong Kong (Dongguan) 3Saint Francis University

Abstract

While recent works have indicated that training-free few-step sampling solvers can achieve nearly quality-lossless acceleration for diffusion and flow-matching models, their real impact on production diffusion generation is not fully understood. In this work, we develop a comprehensive systematization for training-free few-step sampling on diffusion models by enumerating the quality metrics that the literature and industry actually use to judge sampling acceleration. We present a critical analysis under practical production environments, characterizing the realistic quality dimensions a service cares about—human-preference alignment, aesthetic quality, dense instruction following, and in-image long-text rendering—which the standard FID/CLIP protocol overlooks. Our findings are rather surprising: contrary to the established belief that few-step solvers are virtually quality-lossless, we show that they remain near-lossless only on simple distribution-alignment metrics yet degrade substantially under the metrics a production service actually cares about, and thus deliver no effective few-step acceleration. We go even further and propose ParaLin++, a novel state-of-the-art parallel sampling method built on our conference ParaLin, and show via an extensive set of experiments across Stable Diffusion-v2, LSUN Church, FLUX.1-dev, and Ming-Flash-Omni 2.0 that it preserves the full-step Euler quality profile at 3.6×–5.2× (up to 71.4× at 1000 steps), 1.68× on FLUX and 1.21× on Ming.

The Metric Gap

The decisive axis of a training-free accelerator is the metric that certifies it “quality-lossless”, not the speed lever it uses. The academic literature stops at distributional and text-alignment proxies, while production services are judged on a much deeper quality profile:

📚 What academia reports

  • FID / sFID / IS — distribution-to-distribution alignment only
  • CLIP score / CLIP-FID — coarse prompt–image embedding similarity

Outdated for production: blind to sample-level semantics, dense instructions, aesthetics, and in-image text.

🏭 What production requires

  • GenEval — object composition & counting fidelity
  • DPG-Bench — dense, multi-clause prompt following
  • LongTextBench — long, legible in-image text
  • Human-preference & aesthetic alignment

The Problem with Few-Step Solvers

Step-shortening solvers (DDIM, DPMSolver++, UniPC) buy fewer discrete steps by altering the prompt-conditioned trajectory — and the altered trajectory is exactly what breaks dense-prompt and text fidelity; FID merely fails to reflect this break because it overlooks sample-level semantics. Parallel sampling instead keeps all N discrete steps and removes only the solver's temporal dependency. We call this the step-parity route, and ParaLin++ is its strongest realization.

Our Solution: ParaLin++

ParaLin++ reinterprets sequential sampling as a mixed system of linear and nonlinear equations and solves it in parallel. Building on our conference ParaLin, it combines four components:

Adaptive Nonlinearization (ϱ-nonlinear system)

Only the first ϱ blocks stay nonlinear; the rest become linear with reused clean-sample predictions, drastically cutting NFE.

Successive Over-Relaxation

Provably stable for 0 < ω < 2/(1+L), accelerating contraction of both nonlinear and linear blocks.

Sliding Window

Bounds the working set for long trajectories such as 1000-step DDPM.

Adaptive Stopping

A relative historical-error test with an exponential moving average terminates iterations precisely.

Theory: an accumulated error bound relative to the exact sequential trajectory, and convergence of the iteration to the ϱ-nonlinear surrogate system within K ≤ N iterations.

How It Works: The Partially Linear Pipeline

Instead of treating all steps as computationally expensive nonlinear operations, our method linearizes most of the sampling process. As shown in the pipeline below, only a small number of steps (the "Nonlinear Region" ϱ) require full model evaluations. The remaining steps ("Linear Region") are solved with a highly efficient linear solver, dramatically reducing NFE and unleashing parallel efficiency.

Quantitative Results

ParaLin++ sets a new state of the art in accelerating diffusion sampling. On Stable Diffusion-v2 (classifier guidance w = 7.5) and LSUN Church (5000 samples), it consistently achieves the lowest wall-clock time and iteration counts, with negligible change in FID or CLIP scores, even on a single GPU.
